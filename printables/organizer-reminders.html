Kind Reminders for the Organizer

The boring stuff that keeps the fun stuff legal, quiet, and neighborly.

Permits & the city (do this first, dates vary widely)

Check your city/county website for "block party permit" — most are free or cheap
Some places need 2–4 weeks lead time; some want a petition of neighbors' signatures
Street closing may require barricades or notification of local transit/trash pickup
Ask the fire department (non-emergency number) about tent/burner/grill rules
Amplified music often has quiet-hour limits — daytime is safest
Alcohol in public streets is a no in many cities — keep it private-property if allowed, or skip it

Neighbors with different schedules, diets, and lives

Night-shift nurses, truckers, bakers: some neighbors sleep midday — keep midday games outdoors, away from bedroom windows
Deliver a flyer to every house, including the quiet ones and new renters
Offer to pick up or plate a meal for homebound neighbors — an invite that arrives on a plate
Label every dish; keep a copy of ingredient cards next to serving bowls
Have one clearly nut-free, one gluten-optional, and one vegetarian main — one each, labeled, is a kind bar
Sensitive to sensory needs: designate a 20-minute calm zone (porch, shade, bench)

Other things that quietly matter

First-aid basics: bandages, ice pack, one assigned helper "who knows where the ibuprofen is" (that's not medical advice — that's a plan)
Emergency access: keep a driveway/fire lane open, note nearest hydrant
Photograph it! One volunteer with a phone, share in the group thread only — ask before posting faces publicly
Set a shared album OR a printed photo board at next year's party — whatever involves zero apps

The one-sentence version

Feed people clearly, mind the calendar, ask before you amplify, label the food, wave everyone inside if it rains — that's most of it.
